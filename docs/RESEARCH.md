## 3. Published evidence for the strategies

### 3a. Opening Range Breakout (ORB)

**Zarattini & Aziz (2023), "Can Day Trading Really Be Profitable?", [SSRN 4416622](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=4416622).** Instrument **QQQ**, with **TQQQ** used "to circumvent broker leverage constraints"; sample **Jan 2016 – Feb 2023**; cost assumption **USD 0.0005/share commission, no bid-ask spread, no slippage** ([paper PDF mirror](https://static1.squarespace.com/static/5983d931579fb366729580d8/t/643ed6765176b45506e41a01/1681839734183/SSRN-id4416622.pdf), via snippet). Rules as restated by an independent replication: first 5-min bar 09:30–09:35 ET sets direction (bullish → long, bearish → short, doji → no trade), entry at the 09:35 open, **stop at the opening bar's low/high (−1R)**, **target +10R**, otherwise flat at the close; **size = min(1% equity / R, 4× equity / entry)**; USD 25,000 start ([giovannibrusco replication README](https://github.com/giovannibrusco/zarattini-2023-orb-qqq)). An ATR-based floor on R is something I recall from the paper but could not re-read — **UNVERIFIED**. Paper headline figures as cited by the replication: **1,795 trades, Sharpe 1.12, max DD 22%, USD 25k → USD 192,806, CAGR ~31–33%** ([README](https://github.com/giovannibrusco/zarattini-2023-orb-qqq)); hit rate ~24% and +0.13R per trade ([MQL5 replication, snippet](https://www.mql5.com/en/blogs/post/776235)). The eye-catching 9,350% TQQQ-style figure "comes from a parameter search" ([danfin review, snippet](https://danfin.net/opening-range-breakout-research)). Concretum's own page reports "annualized alpha of 33% (net of commissions)" for the QQQ test ([Concretum, snippet](https://concretumgroup.com/can-day-trading-really-be-profitable/)).

**Zarattini, Barbon & Aziz (2024), "A Profitable Day Trading Strategy for the U.S. Equity Market" (Stocks in Play), [SSRN 4729284](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=4729284).** 5-min ORB over **> 7,000 US stocks, 2016–2023**, restricted each day to the **top 20 "Stocks in Play"** by relative volume; claims **> 1,600% total net performance, Sharpe 2.81, annualised alpha 36%** vs 198% for the S&P 500, and says the benefit "holds even after considering transaction costs" ([RePEc abstract, snippet](https://ideas.repec.org/p/chf/rpseri/rp2498.html); [Concretum page](https://concretumgroup.com/a-profitable-day-trading-strategy-for-the-u-s-equity-market/)). The exact commission/slippage assumptions were not retrievable — **UNVERIFIED**.

**Independent replications and what they found**

- **giovannibrusco (GitHub, QQQ 2016–Feb 2023)** — gross result reproduces closely: 1,775 trades, Sharpe 1.06, max DD 22.4%, PnL USD 138,639 = **USD 0.070/share, t = 1.79**, CAGR 30.4% (final USD 164k vs paper's 193k). **Costs kill it**: with USD 0.02/share entry slippage (+0.04 on stops) PnL falls to USD 4,860, Sharpe 0.23, max DD 43.9%; **break-even ≈ 2.2¢/share, about two QQQ spreads**. Only ~2–3% of trades reach +10R, ~75% stop out. 2022 alone is 38% of PnL (76% for the NQ-filtered variant); bootstrap Sharpe CIs overlap buy-and-hold (Sharpe 0.72). Author's verdict: "the edge lives inside the bid-ask spread", single-regime, volatility-linked ([repo](https://github.com/giovannibrusco/zarattini-2023-orb-qqq)).
- **MQL5 blog (25 Sep 2026), five indices** — gross figures reproduce; the paper "charges commission but no spread and no slippage" and has no out-of-sample period; **net result after costs: zero** ([post, snippet](https://www.mql5.com/en/blogs/post/776235)).
- **QuantConnect, Stocks in Play (2016 only)** — Sharpe 2.4 vs 0.84 for SPY, near-zero beta, 17 of 25 parameter combinations beat the benchmark; universe = 1,000 most liquid US stocks > USD 5 with ATR > USD 0.50; **fees/slippage not stated** ([QuantConnect, snippet](https://www.quantconnect.com/research/18444/opening-range-breakout-for-stocks-in-play/)).
- Nothing independent covering **2023–2026** was found for either paper; the replication author calls extending the sample "the highest-value next test" ([repo](https://github.com/giovannibrusco/zarattini-2023-orb-qqq)). r/algotrading threads: none surfaced in search.

### 3b. Market intraday momentum

- **Gao, Han, Li & Zhou (2018), JFE 129(2): 394–414, [SSRN 2440866](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=2440866).** SPY 1993–2013: the first half-hour return (from the prior close) predicts the last half-hour return; stronger on volatile, high-volume, recession and macro-news days; also present in ten other ETFs ([EconPapers](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:eee:jfinec:v:129:y:2018:i:2:p:394-414)). Magnitude (working-paper version, SPY 1999–2012): **R² ≈ 0.02** (0.035 adding the 12th half-hour), out-of-sample R² 0.018; the timing rule (hold SPY in the last half-hour only when the first half-hour was positive) earns a **gross 6.3%/yr** vs −0.5% for always holding the last half-hour; a later check on Aug 2012–May 2014 found R² 0.014 ([CXO Advisory](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/calendar-effects/first-and-last-half-hours-of-trading-linked/)). Alpha Architect quotes 6.34%/yr but adds "we find no evidence to support this result from our in-house backtests" ([Alpha Architect](https://alphaarchitect.com/attention-prop-traders-the-first-half-hour-of-trading-predicts-the-last-half-hour/)). **Net-of-cost figures: not found → UNVERIFIED.** The rule implies roughly one round trip per trading day, so any per-trade cost above a few bp consumes most of 6%/yr.
- **Baltussen, Da, Lammers & Martens (2021), JFE 142(1): 377–403, [SSRN 3760365](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=3760365).** > 60 futures (equities, bonds, commodities, currencies), 1974–2020: the last-30-minute return is predicted by the rest-of-day return and reverses over following days; attributed to gamma hedging by option market makers and leveraged ETFs ([SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=3760365); [author PDF](https://academicweb.nd.edu/~zda/intramom.pdf)). Net-of-cost results **UNVERIFIED**; a third-party backtest site reports small, inconsistent returns (−0.90% to +2.45%/yr) ([paperswithbacktest](https://paperswithbacktest.com/strategies/s-lammers-m-martens-low-risk-effect-hedging-demand-and-market-intraday-momentum)) — unverified.
- **Zarattini, Aziz & Barbon (2024), "Beat the Market", [SSRN 4824172](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=4824172).** SPY 2007–early 2024, "noise area" bands around the open, breakout entries with VWAP-based trailing stops, EOD exit: **1,985% total return net of costs, 19.6%/yr, Sharpe 1.33** ([RePEc, snippet](https://ideas.repec.org/p/chf/rpseri/rp2497.html); [Concretum](https://concretumgroup.com/beat-the-market-an-effective-intraday-momentum-strategy-for-sp500-etf-spy/)). Concretum's own code uses commission USD 0.0035/share, min USD 0.35 ([Concretum MATLAB post, snippet](https://concretumgroup.com/backtesting-riding-intraday-trends-in-us-markets-using-matlab/)). **Replications:** codecat-ops reports Sharpe 1.11 and alpha +16.7%/yr (t 2.85) vs the paper's 1.33/19.6%, a **trade-level edge of only ~+2.6 bp per trade at a 41% win rate**, and that **the edge has compressed since 2025 to a Sharpe near zero on both SPY and ES, with walk-forward optimisation destroying value** ([search extract](https://github.com/codecat-ops/zarattini-2024-momentum-spy) — the repo URL returned 404 when fetched, so **UNVERIFIED**). svettelnova replicates on 30-min Alpaca SIP bars 2016–Oct 2026 with USD 0.0035/share commission + USD 0.001/share slippage and a 2016–21 / 2022–26 split, but publishes no numbers ([repo](https://github.com/svettelnova/intraday-momentum-spy)). A follow-up SSRN paper on parameter optimisation exists but was not read ([Maróy, SSRN 5095349](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/Delivery.cfm/5095349.pdf?abstractid=5095349&mirid=1)).

### 3c. VWAP mean reversion on liquid ETFs

No academic paper on intraday VWAP mean reversion in SPY/ETFs was found. Practitioner evidence is weak and mixed: a SPY test (Sep 2012–Jan 2018) found a 27% win rate for the buy-above-VWAP variant and performance "degraded by transaction costs" at USD 0.005/share ([StockSoft Research](https://stocksoftresearch.com/vwap-trading-system/)); a self-published ES-futures study claims a 0.89-pp mean-reversion edge vs 0.10–0.15 pp costs but **zero significant results across 74,800 VWAP-crossover configurations** ([TradingView post](https://www.tradingview.com/chart/ES1!/tVJcD92K-Everyone-Uses-VWAP-Wrong/)); a vendor's Q1-2025 SPY result (+8.6%, Sharpe 2.8) mixes VWAP with volatility bands, overnight gaps and 4× leverage, so it is not a VWAP test ([EdgeForAlpha](https://edgeforalpha.com/insights/spy-vwap-vol-bands-q1-2025)). **Verdict: no credible after-cost evidence at retail scale.**

## 4. Verdict

**Costs that matter for a £100–£5,000 account**

1. **Spread/slippage** — the ORB replication's break-even is **2.2¢/share** on QQQ, and the best-documented SPY intraday-momentum edge is **~2–3 bp per trade**; both are inside normal retail execution noise. Market-order-only live execution on Trading 212 means the bot pays the spread twice and cannot hold a server-side stop.
2. **FX fee** — Trading 212's **0.15% per conversion** exceeds 2.2¢/share for any share priced above ~USD 15, *per side*. Unless USD is held in a multi-currency Invest account **and** the API can trade from it (UNVERIFIED, one SDK says it cannot), US-listed day trades on T212 are dead on arrival.
3. **Commissions** — IBKR's **USD 1.00 minimum per order** = USD 2 round trip = 2% of a USD 100 account, 0.2% of USD 1,000, 0.04% of USD 5,000.
4. **Data fees** — IBKR's USD 10/month US bundle (waived only at ≥ USD 30 commissions, i.e. ≥ 30 orders/month) or GBP 4.25–5/month for LSE L1 is several percent per month of a £100 account and roughly 0.1–0.2% per month of £5,000.
5. **Instrument access** — SPY/QQQ/TQQQ are not buyable for UK retail; UCITS substitutes (CSPX/VUSA/EQQQ) trade LSE hours in GBP with (UNVERIFIED) wider spreads and no verified free intraday history. US single stocks are available on both brokers.

**Blunt answer.** At **£100–£1,000, probably none** of these strategies survive: fixed costs (data, USD 1 minimums, or the 0.15% FX layer) are an order of magnitude larger than the per-trade edges that independent replications measure. At **£5,000 on IBKR Pro** the cost stack falls to a few bp per round trip plus ~USD 10/month, which is the *only* combination in this report where a thin edge is arithmetically possible — and even then the two most-replicated strategies (ORB on the Nasdaq-100 ETF; Beat-the-Market on SPY) show post-2022 decay and net-zero results in independent tests. The one paper with large claimed *net* alpha (Stocks in Play, Sharpe 2.81) requires scanning thousands of US single stocks for relative volume, 1-min data for many symbols, shorting, and fast fills — the opposite of a "simple" bot, and its cost assumptions could not be verified. Best-chance configuration if you proceed anyway: **IBKR Pro cash account, US single stocks or SPY-equivalents via UCITS, bracket orders, trading only on high-volatility days (the regime both ORB replications attribute the edge to), £5,000 not £100**, after a backtest that charges at least 2¢/share + USD 1 per order and a demo period on Trading 212's sandbox purely to test the plumbing. Treat anything that only wins with zero slippage as not winning.
